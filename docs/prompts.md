# Prompts

**Every challenge the app can draw, current and archived.**
**Last updated:** 2026-09-08
**Source of truth:** `src/domain/prompts.ts` — this document mirrors it, and both change together.

Nothing here is ever deleted. When a deck is rewritten the old wording is archived, kept in the
app as a selectable bundle, and written down below. That way a change can be compared rather than
taken on faith — run a night on each and ask the dancers which they preferred.

---

## How prompts are written

Three rules, all of which came out of the first real event (2026-09-02), where the originals played
as too strict and the operator softened one out loud mid-dance.

### 1. Invite, do not demand
*As many as you can* and *as little as you can* beat *every* and *must*. A prompt is a direction to
explore, not a test to pass — and a social has mixed levels, so everybody has to be able to take
part. The original **Anchor Detective** demanded every anchor differ, which is genuinely hard; it
is now **Anchor Hunt**, which asks how many you can find.

### 2. Add a limit only where the prompt is exploitable
If a lazy reading satisfies the prompt with no effort, it gets a guard rail. Otherwise it stays
open. Three in the current deck need one:

| Prompt | The lazy reading | The guard |
|---|---|---|
| **Small World** | Stand still — that is very small | "Keep dancing though — standing still is small, but it is not a dance." |
| **One Hand** | Never connect at all | "Stay connected — dropping the connection is not one-handed, it is no-handed." |
| **Slow Motion** | Stop moving | "Keep moving — stopping is not slow, it is stopped." |

**The worked example** the owner gave, for prompts not yet written: a challenge about covering the
most distance is trivially won by tandem-walking the whole song, so it would need something like
*"no more than four walking steps in a row"*. Write the guard against the specific cheat, not as a
general rule.

### 3. Short names
Names are read off a wheel segment. At a full pool roughly **twelve characters** survive before the
label truncates, so names are kept short and the description carries the detail.

## Westie Starter Pack — current

**20 prompts · West Coast Swing · in use by default**

| Name | Description |
|---|---|
| **Level Up** | Work in as many level changes as you can. The more drastic, the better. |
| **Anchor Hunt** | See how many different anchors you can find. Variety is the point — nobody is counting. |
| **Slow Motion** | Stretch as much as you can and take your time getting anywhere. Keep moving — stopping is not slow, it is stopped. |
| **Hit It** | Catch as many breaks in the music as you can, together. Silence counts too. |
| **Whip It** | Build as much of the dance around whips as you can manage. |
| **Hands Off** | See how often you can break the connection and find your way back without reaching. Coming back is the hard half. |
| **Swap Roles** | Trade who is leading as often as you like, and make the handovers as smooth as you can. |
| **Curve It** | Travel with as few straight lines as you can. Curve whatever will curve. |
| **Big Faces** | Give the back row as much expression as you can. Dance it with your eyebrows if you like. |
| **Fancy Feet** | Put in as much footwork as you can survive. Syncopate whatever you fancy. |
| **One Hand** | Pick a hand and see how long you can keep to it. Stay connected — dropping the connection is not one-handed, it is no-handed. |
| **Freeze Frame** | Freeze at the top of as many phrases as you can, holding a beat longer than feels comfortable. Dance the rest of the phrase. |
| **Sing It** | Dance the words as much as the beat. If the singer says it, show it — sillier is better. |
| **Roll It** | Live in the and-counts as much as you can, landing squarely on a number as little as you can. |
| **Small World** | Use as little of the floor as you can. Keep dancing though — standing still is small, but it is not a dance. |
| **Arrive Late** | Delay as many arrivals as you can. Get there late, on purpose, and together. |
| **Shine Time** | Take as many solo moments as you like, and react as much as you can when it is not your turn. |
| **Push & Float** | Move between grounded and floating as much as you can. Let the room see the difference. |
| **Mirror** | Echo each other's shapes as often as you can. Close enough is close enough. |
| **Silent Movie** | Tell as much of the story as you can with your body alone. No words, no mouthing along. |

## Westie Pack (original) — archived

**20 prompts · West Coast Swing · selectable in the Prompt Bank**

The first wording, written before the app had ever run an event. Kept so the two can be compared
directly. Stricter throughout: more rules, fewer invitations.

| Name | Description |
|---|---|
| **A Whole New Level** | Add as many level changes to your dance as you can. The more drastic the level change, the better. |
| **Anchor Detective** | Every anchor has to look different from the last one. Repeat an anchor and you owe the room a bow. |
| **Slow Motion** | Stretch everything. Take twice as long as feels natural to get anywhere, and arrive together anyway. |
| **Hit the Breaks** | Find every break in the music and hit it together. Silence is a move — use it. |
| **Whip It Good** | Build the whole dance around whips. Every pattern must arrive at one or leave from one. |
| **Hands Off** | Break the connection at least three times and find your way back without reaching for each other. |
| **Swap the Reins** | Trade who is leading at least twice, and make the handover invisible to anyone watching. |
| **The Long Way Round** | No pattern may travel in a straight line down the slot. Curve absolutely everything. |
| **Say It With Your Face** | Every phrase gets an expression the back row can read. Dance it with your eyebrows. |
| **Footwork Flurry** | Trade the simplest footwork you know for the busiest you can survive. Syncopate everything. |
| **One Handed** | Pick a hand at the start of the song. That is the only hand you connect with for the whole dance. |
| **Stop and Stare** | Freeze completely at the top of every phrase, and hold it one beat longer than is comfortable. |
| **Play the Lyrics** | Dance the words, not the beat. If the singer says it, show it — the sillier the better. |
| **Rolling Count Riot** | Live in the and-counts. Try never to land squarely on a number for the whole song. |
| **Small World** | The whole dance happens in a slot half its normal length. Nobody may step outside it. |
| **Take the Scenic Route** | Delay every arrival. Get everywhere late, on purpose, and get there at the same moment. |
| **Shine Time** | Each of you takes at least one solo moment while the other stops and openly reacts to it. |
| **Heavy and Light** | Alternate every eight counts between grounded, weighted movement and light, floating movement. |
| **Mirror Match** | Whatever one of you does, the other answers with the same shape before the phrase ends. |
| **Silent Movie** | Tell the entire story with your body. No smiling with your mouth — everything else is fair game. |

## Redrawing

If a couple gets a challenge that does not suit them, **Tools → Draw a different challenge** spins
the wheel again with the current one taken off it, so the draw plainly offers something else. The
replaced challenge is not spent — it returns to the pool once that couple is committed.

## Testing the rewrite

Both bundles are in the app. In the **Prompt Bank**, pick a bundle and press **Use this bundle for
sessions** — the next session draws from it, and the roster screen shows which one is in play before
you start. **View all Prompts** shows every challenge in one list, and can also be selected to draw
from both bundles at once — though that mixes the wordings together, so it is best left until the
comparison is finished. Run a night on each and ask the dancers which they
preferred. Set-asides are stored per prompt, so hiding one in either bundle does not disturb the
other.
